As a technical writer, I occasionally get the opportunity to join our project manager on customer implementations, supporting the project and co-training users. It gives me valuable exposure to the processes behind the technology I document.

<!-- truncate -->

One observation from these engagements has surprised me: **many logistics processes are still remarkably manual.**

Even in large corporations, paper remains part of day-to-day logistics operations. In some cases, spreadsheets represent a significant level of digitalisation.

Perhaps it shouldn't be surprising. Large organisations have established processes, multiple teams and many users to consider. Introducing new technology takes time—not necessarily because of resistance to change, but because change at that scale requires careful decisions and coordination.

Seeing this first-hand also puts our work into perspective.

We're not simply implementing new technology. We're helping organisations move long-established processes into a more digital way of working.

As a technical writer, being involved beyond the documentation—understanding existing processes, engaging with users and helping train them on new ones—makes the impact of the work much more tangible.

It's rewarding to be part of that transformation.
